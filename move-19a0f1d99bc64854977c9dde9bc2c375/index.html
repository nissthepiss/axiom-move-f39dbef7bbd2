The one-minute version

You were right to ask whether this had become a rabbit hole.

The last two days were not spent rebuilding one viewer or scraper. They were spent mapping the existing machine, writing the future rules for the entire system, and making two independent reviewers attack the same frozen plan before it decides what server you buy.

The uncomfortable truth: too much elapsed time went into safely packaging the reviewers’ answers. That protects the decision, but it is review machinery—not the migration itself. We have not started the Linux implementation phase.

02

The kind of question this must make easy

“Find every coin that hit $10k, then $50k. What patterns stand out?”

Include its complete chart shape, wallets, holders, known KOL presence, Phanes ranking history, and Axiom viewer signals across a year or more—without waiting days for data preparation.

1

Find the cohort cheaply

A small lifecycle index finds the coins that crossed both levels and records exactly when.

2

Read only what matters

Load those coins’ chart windows plus matching wallet, holder, KOL, Phanes, and Axiom facts.

3

Use what was known then

Rankings and memberships are time-aware, so future knowledge cannot leak into an old event.

4

Analyse in AxiomLab

The experiment keeps the question, recipe, fixed data snapshot, result, and route back to the original facts.

Training-ready snapshots?

Yes—but thin ones.

Store the expensive raw facts once. Compute shared indexes and reusable features once. Each experiment saves a compact feature-and-label table plus a manifest pointing back to the source. Large derived caches may expire because they can be rebuilt. We do not copy every coin’s full history into every experiment.

03

Why two days disappeared

Two very different kinds of problem got mixed together.

Worth finding

Real holes in the future architecture

Reviewers found places where missing data could look complete, safety actions could collide with the normal record order, identity could drift, storage evidence could be weaker than it appeared, or research could stop being reproducible.

Those findings affect whether the future system can be trusted. They belong before the purchase.

The frustrating delay

New shapes in the reviewer’s message stream

The reviewer sometimes completed a valid answer but packaged failed tools or the final message in a shape the strict reader had never seen. The reader refused to guess, so each newly observed shape needed a tiny versioned rule and hostile tests.

The latest answer is complete. It is being recovered without asking the model to repeat or rewrite it.

What the machinery now guarantees

Failure stays failure. Success must be whole.

  • A failed review can never occupy the official success filenames.
  • A crash halfway through publication cannot leave a believable half-result.
  • Every answer is tied to the exact plan, code, model, and repository version that produced it.
  • Windows line-ending changes can no longer make clean files look altered.
  • New message shapes are rejected safely and admitted only through a narrow tested rule.
  • The current answer can be recovered with no new model call and no edited report text.

What remains before you buy the server

Four understandable checkpoints.

01

Finish the no-model recovery

Import the already-completed answer under the newly tested message rule. Preserve its original failure evidence. Do not ask the reviewer to rewrite history.

02

Repair the five serious findings

Apply the review’s five material findings to the architecture. Explicitly mark Olympus as later and non-blocking.

03

Get two clean independent checks

Both reviewers must find no new serious issue in the same unchanged design. A genuine new issue is fixed and the clean count starts again.

04

Hand you the exact purchase sheet

Vendor, model, RAM, disks, location, setup and monthly cost, storage layout, security baseline, and the first-day build sequence.

Honest estimate from this checkpoint

Best path: about 4–8 focused hours

That is a range, not a promise. The recovery itself should be well under an hour. Most uncertainty is whether the repaired architecture survives both new attacks. A real problem will be fixed rather than waived to protect the estimate.
