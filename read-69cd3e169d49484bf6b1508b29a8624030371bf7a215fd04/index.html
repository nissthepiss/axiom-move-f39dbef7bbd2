Yes, it could, and you are right to raise it. Everything you describe is real: the completed bar that leaks its own future, the rolling window that looks both ways, the normaliser that has already seen 2026, the fill at a price that did not exist yet. Any of them can make a worthless model look brilliant, and the fast, event-level data we want to exploit makes the mistake easier to hide, not harder.

Most of what you raise has been central to the migration design from the start, rather than a patch added after your note. The design, as written, keeps separate clocks for when something happened on chain, when we received it, and when it was durably written down; it refuses to let a label learned later describe an earlier moment; it keeps outcomes in separate custody from decisions. Your note exposed four places where that thinking was implied but not yet written down as law: when bars and features become usable, what data a fitted statistic saw, how related observations are separated between train and test, and how a paper fill is allowed to be priced. Those four repairs are now written into the candidate Constitution, architecture and acceptance ledger.

None of this is solved. Written is not proven. Every acceptance proof that would demonstrate these laws hold in practice is still marked not_started, the six months of tape we already have was not captured under these laws, and no walk-forward test or latency sweep has been run. This page is the ledger of exactly what is designed, what runs today, what proof is owed, and what remains a later study.

The now line: what a decision at time T may know

Read left to right. Every fact carries its own clocks, and the model at T may use only what was durably known by T. Everything to the right of the cut belongs to the decision, execution or outcome audit and cannot feed back into that decision.

On a narrow screen this line reads top to bottom in the same order.

  1. EventIt happened on chainSlot, block, transaction index. Ordered by the chain, never by our wall clock.
  2. ReceivedWe saw itOur receipt time, with a measured clock-uncertainty bound for that host and boot.
  3. DurableIt was written downA first-durable sequence and time, assigned by the database, never by the writer.
  4. Cut at TKnowledge cutoffOnly facts and labels durable by T may be read. An open bar stays explicitly open; it is closed only after its closure and watermark were available.
  5. DecideDecision receiptWhat was read, which version of which recipe, and when. Sealed before any outcome is seen.
  6. FillEarliest eligible fillDecision time plus a declared latency, priced only from evidence that existed by then.
  7. OutcomeWhat happened nextHeld in separate custody. Used to score, never to build a feature.

Usable before the decisionFacts, bars and labels whose own availability time is at or before T.

Forbidden to the modelAnything later. It may only be used to judge what the decision did.

02

What we build

What we are actually building: five layers, each with its own clock.

Think of it as a record office rather than a spreadsheet. Facts arrive, get stamped at the door, get filed once, and are only ever summarised by recipes with batch numbers. Decisions are sealed in envelopes before the result is known, and the results are kept by a separate clerk. The plain description comes first on each layer; the technical label underneath is what the documents call it.

  1. The sealed shoebox of receipts

    Every relevant thing that happened is kept once, exactly as it arrived, and never edited. That includes attempts that landed and failed, which today's system throws away, plus the exact chain coordinates, status and error, fee, compute units and the user's own price limits where the chain supplies them. Off-chain messages are kept per source, in their original shape.

    Technical: cold truth. Immutable L0 bytes; execution_attempt_v1 for landed_success and landed_failed; source-specific external_observation_envelope_v1; versioned protocol decoders for native constraints such as minimum_amount_out. DC-02, DC-08.

    If a byte was never captured, it is marked absent. It is never reconstructed from a later price.

  2. The date stamp at the door

    Each receipt gets several stamps: when it happened on chain, when we received it (with a measured error bar for our clock), and when the database durably wrote it down. The writer cannot choose the last one. An import can claim an old event date, but it cannot pretend we knew it earlier than we did.

    Technical: event/source time, receipt time with clock_calibration_v1, database-assigned first_durable_seq and first_durable_at, and knowledge_cutoff_v1 for every read. Availability can move forward by amendment, never backward. DC-03, DC-08.

    Two receipts from different hosts two seconds apart compare as before, after, overlaps or unknown. Overlapping error bars are never called a lead.

  3. Recipes with batch numbers

    Bars, averages, counts and scalers are made from the shoebox by named recipes. Every batch says which recipe version, which inputs, and up to which moment it is valid. A bar says whether it is still open or closed and when its last ingredient arrived. A fitted statistic says which rows it was fitted on and cannot be used before that period ends.

    Technical: warm shared releases with derivation ids hashing code, parameters, inputs and cutoff (DC-13). Bars and features carry interval start and end, open_partial|closed, watermark and per-row available_at. fitted_artifact_v1 binds fit rows, release and fit cutoff. Engine parity is checked three ways before a release is trusted (DC-12).

    Two experiments asking for the same batch share one copy. Nobody gets a private version of the truth.

  4. The sealed envelope before the race

    When a decision is made, or a candidate is evaluated and refused, a receipt records what was read, at which cutoff, with which recipe versions, and what the answer was. A paper fill is then allowed only at decision time plus a declared delay, at a price that existed by then. The envelope is sealed before anyone looks at what happened next.

    Technical: decision_evaluation_v1 terminal receipts for every evaluated candidate, including refusals and no-signal outcomes; paper_fill_contract_v1 binding decision receipt, latency, earliest eligible fill frontier and causal price evidence. DC-13, MIG-ACC-000037, MIG-ACC-000043.

    A failed landed attempt is evidence of an attempt. It is never turned into a trade, a fill or a balance change.

  5. Scores kept by a separate referee

    Outcomes live with a separate clerk who only answers "what happened after T" through a recorded query. Holdout data is counted out like a ration and cannot be refilled by renaming a study. Real landed fills, paper fills, counterfactual "what if we had followed" fills and claims from a video stay in four different drawers.

    Technical: sealed experiment records before outcome access, counted holdout custody, outcome_access_receipt_v1, immutable score rows with visible re-score amendments. Actual versus paper versus counterfactual versus video outcomes are distinct through replay. DC-10, DC-13.

    A score can be superseded in public. It can never be quietly rewritten.

Why this is the whole point of the migrationThe move is not mainly about a faster machine. It is about being able to ask hundreds of future questions without collecting the past again and without each experiment inventing its own truth. Re-capture is impossible for bytes that were never kept, and a private copy of history per experiment is how five experiments end up disagreeing about one Tuesday.

Capture once, stamp honestly, derive by versioned recipe, decide with a sealed receipt, score through a referee. Every study below is the same substrate asked a different question.

Why this unlocks many reliable experiments.

Thirteen studies the substrate is designed to serve. Each names what a decision at T may know and what stays outcome-only. These are studies that become askable, not edges that have been found. Any of them may find nothing.

04

Storage

More data without a memory or storage explosion.

Capturing failed attempts, fee and compute detail, user price limits and per-source off-chain messages means more bytes than today. The design absorbs that with one rule repeated at three temperatures: keep it once, share the summaries, bound the hot set. The picture is a library, not a photocopier.

One archive copycold truth
Every relevant envelope is stored once, compressed and sealed. Experiments reference its release and chunk ids; they do not copy it. If a byte is needed again, it is read from the same shelf.
A shared indexwarm, content-addressed
Bars, participation prefixes, rapid-path chunks and fitted artifacts are built by recipe and named by their content. Thirty-five overlapping requests share one materialisation rather than making thirty-five. Everything here rebuilds from cold truth.
A small reading deskhot, bounded
A fixed-size ring of recent slots, plus exact state only for mints that have been deliberately promoted. A lossy sketch may nominate a candidate for promotion; it can never supply a count, a rate or a decision value.
No private historiesno per-experiment copies
An experiment keeps a manifest naming the exact slice it used, not the slice. This is what stops five experiments disagreeing about one afternoon.
Hard ceilingscaps and refusal
Resident memory, queue depth, spool, active mints, slot tail and tracked identities each have a cap. When a cap is hit, admission is refused or closed with a receipt, or the interval is marked unavailable. Nothing is ever silently sampled to fit.

How the machine size will be decidedCapacity is proven, not estimated: two representative live hours of real input plus a replay of the measured peak day freeze the bytes per day, queue, spool, memory, active-mint, slot-tail and identity caps before any hot projection is admitted. The exact bill of materials comes out of that measurement. It is not guessed on this page and it has not been produced yet.

05

Legacy tape

What six months of legacy tape can and cannot answer.

The current system has recorded roughly six months of market history. That tape is valuable and it is not being thrown away. It is also narrower than the target, and honesty about the gap is the only way it stays useful.

It can support

  • Exploratory questions about successful landed trades, prices and participation as they were recorded.
  • Forming hypotheses that a later, properly captured study would then test.
  • Shape-of-the-market context: how bursts, graduations and quiet periods looked across half a year.
  • Anchoring future capture: knowing which events to replay to test the new laws.

It cannot support

  • Any certified failure-rate or attempt-pressure study from this tape alone. Failed landed transactions were filtered out at capture; a historical reconstruction would need a separately qualified chain-history source and is not assumed.
  • Exact fee, compute-unit and native price-limit analysis where those fields were never kept.
  • A certified leak-free feature set. Legacy bars and fill logic have not been checked under the new availability and fill laws.
  • A realistic latency sweep. Without failed attempts and exact coordinates the "would we have landed" half of the question is missing.

The rule that does not bendMissing old bytes cannot be invented. The tape keeps its legacy, uncertified status, and a conclusion drawn from it is labelled as drawn from it. Re-labelling does not certify anything.

What changed

What changed because of this review.

An independent audit of the current design against your note found that most of it was already law and that four contracts were implied but not written precisely enough. They are now written into the candidate Constitution, architecture and acceptance ledger as MIG-ACC-000043. Its proof status is not_started. Latency sweeps remain a later research exercise, not another migration contract.

Repair 1: bars declare when they existcausal_bar_feature_v1
Every bar and feature row carries interval start and end, open_partial or closed, a watermark, and a per-row available_at that waits for every input plus required closure evidence. feature_usable_at then also waits until the derived row itself is durably written. A completed current bar cannot be read before those waits pass; centered and forward windows remain target-only and never enter a decision-feature release. Design
Repair 2: fitted statistics are pinnedfitted_artifact_v1
Any mean, standard deviation, scaler or threshold records the rows and release it was fitted on and a fit cutoff. It cannot be applied to a decision earlier than that cutoff, so a whole-dataset normaliser cannot reach backward. Design
Repair 3: paper fills need evidencepaper_fill_contract_v1
A paper fill binds its decision receipt, a declared latency, the earliest eligible fill frontier and the causal price evidence it used. Filling at the decision bar's close, at a future low, or at the best price in a window is forbidden. Design
Repair 4: related rows stay on one side of the examexperiment_split_plan_v1
Every promotable experiment declares a causal, chronological or walk-forward split, the keys that keep related observations together, and enough purge or embargo around the boundary to cover the lookback and outcome horizon. Random temporal splits stay exploratory. Design
Research policy, not a gateregistered latency sweeps
Latency sweeps at +0 to +1000 ms are a standing robustness study after the causal fill contract exists. They are not conditions for buying the server and none has been run. Later study

Evidence

Technical evidence and stable identifiers.

Collapsed by default. These are the internal identifiers the statements on this page rest on, so that any line above can be traced to a law or an acceptance item. Internal file locations stay internal.

Data Constitution laws referenced
DC-02complete source capture
"Maximal source" is a set, not one stream. Declares successful and landed-failed coverage; execution_attempt_v1 binds signature, coordinates, status and error, requested and actual compute and fee. Names today's successful-only filter as a legacy gap. A constraint is never called realised slippage.
DC-03immutability and knowledge time
Sealed bytes never change; corrections append. Event time and first-known time are separate; imports cannot backdate knowledge; availability moves forward only. The database assigns first_durable_seq and first_durable_at; writer-supplied times are treated as claims.
DC-08time, forks, ordering
Slot, rooted block, transaction index, observed, received, decoded and sealed times, commitment state and protocol generation are all stored. Chain ordering never uses a wall clock. Per-host, per-boot clock_calibration_v1; cross-host comparison returns before, after, overlaps or unknown.
DC-10completeness and certification
A certification domain never spans a protocol transition; manifests record expected versus observed counts, gaps, orphans and quarantine. Sealed and certified are distinct states; readers see certified manifests only.
DC-12engine parity
Every decision-bearing query must produce identical results through the pinned engine, the same engine with optimisations disabled, and an independent reader. A mismatch quarantines both the engine build and the finding.
DC-13derivation, custody, scores
Derivation ids hash code, parameters, dependency closure, input release and cutoff. Experiments are sealed before outcome access; holdout consumption is counted and cannot be reset by renaming. Scores are immutable rows with visible supersession. The live scorer reaches outcomes only through a certified broker.
DC-17crash safety and capacity
At capacity, research stops first, sealing pauses second, capture stops loudly with a declared hole before any unverified write. Every long-lived projection declares caps, admission priority, eviction receipts and a bounded re-entry path; no selector scans everything seen since boot.
Acceptance items referenced, all not_started
MIG-ACC-000037Gate 2
One durable terminal decision_evaluation_v1 receipt per evaluated candidate, including refused and no-signal; zero receipts for unevaluated universe members; distinct states survive crash, restart and outcome join.
MIG-ACC-000039Gate 2
Cross-module answers bind observed_chain_frontier_v1, canonical_resolution_v1 and knowledge_cutoff_v1; off-chain components join only as latest trusted durable no later than the decision cutoff. Fixtures include same-slot multi-instruction, 33-by-1 versus 1-by-33, bitemporal label gaps and two viewer frames straddling one trade.
MIG-ACC-000040Gate 2
Every long-lived projection and serving object is bounded, shared, evictable and exactly rebuildable. Thirty-five overlapping requests within 1.25 times one materialisation; a large cold identity set with a small active set inspects a bounded selector; a pin beyond capacity is refused before opening.
MIG-ACC-000042Gate 2
The bounded, replayable frenzy-research substrate: exact success and landed-failed denominator, native constraints with units and protocol generation, derived slippage unavailable without an as-known quote, rapid_mint_path_v1 across seams, clock-overlap negatives, and a two-hour live plus peak-day replay capacity receipt before hot admission.
MIG-ACC-000043Gate 2 · not_started
Bar and feature availability (open_partial|closed, watermark, available_at), fitted_artifact_v1 fit-cutoff binding, a causal split plan, and paper_fill_contract_v1 with forbidden fill bases. Written from this review; no implementation proof has run.
Contracts and terms used above
knowledge_cutoff_v1
The durable sequence and time a read is allowed to know up to. Separate from chain frontier.
correlated_read_v1
A read that joins chain-derived components at their observed or canonical frontier and off-chain observations as the latest durable evidence no later than the cutoff, never calling them simultaneous.
causal_bar_feature_v1
A bar or feature row with explicit interval, boundary, watermark/finality and input frontier; its usable time can be no earlier than both its true availability and its own first-durable receipt.
open_partial | closed
Whether a bar's interval has ended as of the watermark. An open bar may be read as open; it may never be read as if closed.
fitted_artifact_v1
Any fitted statistic, bound to fit rows, release and fit cutoff; inapplicable before that cutoff.
paper_fill_contract_v1
Binds decision receipt, latency, earliest eligible fill frontier and causal price evidence for a simulated fill.
landed_success | landed_failed | orphaned | unresolved
Attempt states. A landed failure is an attempt, never a trade. Orphaned and unresolved are observed but not certified canonical.
Holdout custody
Outcome data reserved for confirmatory tests, consumed by count, not refillable by renaming a study.
experiment_split_plan_v1
A preregistered causal split: normally train on an earlier period and test on a later one, group related observations, and purge or embargo the boundary so one burst cannot sit on both sides.
